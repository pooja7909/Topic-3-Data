3.1.1 — Why Computers Use Binary
▼

A computer is an electronic device. At its most basic level, a computer uses electrical signals to store and process information.

An electrical signal can be in one of two states:

  • ON (high voltage) = represented as 1
  • OFF (low voltage) = represented as 0

This is why computers use the binary number system — it has only two digits: 0 and 1. Each 0 or 1 is called a bit (binary digit).

DEFINITION

Bit: A binary digit — either 0 or 1. The smallest unit of information in a computer.

Byte: A group of 8 bits. The standard unit for measuring computer storage.

💡 Why not use denary (base-10)? Representing 10 different voltage levels would be much harder to distinguish reliably. With just two levels (ON/OFF), signals are clear, distinct, and less prone to error.

🎯 What Does Binary Represent?

Binary is the universal representation for ALL data in a computer:

  • Numbers: Integers and decimals are stored as binary
  • Text: Letters are encoded as binary (ASCII, Unicode)
  • Images: Each pixel's color is a binary number
  • Sound: Audio samples are binary numbers
  • Instructions: CPU instructions are binary codes

This is fundamental: Everything a computer stores or processes is binary.

3.1.2 — Binary to Denary Conversion
▼

The denary number system is what we use daily — it has 10 digits (0–9) and is base-10. Each position represents a power of 10.

Similarly, in binary (base-2), each position represents a power of 2.

🔢 Powers of 2 (for 8 bits):

Bit Position Power of 2 Value
7 (leftmost) 27 128
6 26 64
5 25 32
4 24 16
3 23 8
2 22 4
1 21 2
0 (rightmost) 20 1

📋 How to Convert Binary to Denary:

Example: 10110101 → ?

1. Write the powers of 2 above each bit:

128 64 32 16 8 4 2 1
1 0 1 1 0 1 0 1

2. Where there's a 1, add up that power of 2:

128 + 32 + 16 + 4 + 1 = 181

Answer: 10110101 in binary = 181 in denary

✏️ Quick tip: Remember the powers: 128, 64, 32, 16, 8, 4, 2, 1. If you memorize these, binary conversion becomes quick!
3.1.3 — Denary to Binary Conversion
▼

Converting FROM denary TO binary uses the "divide by 2" method:

📋 How to Convert Denary to Binary:

Example: 181 → ?

Repeatedly divide by 2, tracking the remainder (0 or 1):

181 ÷ 2 = 90 remainder 1
90 ÷ 2 = 45 remainder 0
45 ÷ 2 = 22 remainder 1
22 ÷ 2 = 11 remainder 0
11 ÷ 2 = 5 remainder 1
5 ÷ 2 = 2 remainder 1
2 ÷ 2 = 1 remainder 0
1 ÷ 2 = 0 remainder 1

Read remainders from bottom to top: 10110101

Answer: 181 in denary = 10110101 in binary

✏️ Common mistake: Reading the remainders from top to bottom instead of bottom to top. Always read from bottom to top!

📐 Using Powers of 2 Method (Alternative):

Example: 181 → ?

1. Find the largest power of 2 that fits: 128 fits in 181 → put 1 in position 7

2. Remainder: 181 − 128 = 53

3. Next power: 64 doesn't fit in 53 → put 0 in position 6

4. Continue: 32 fits → remainder 21 → 16 fits → remainder 5 → 8 doesn't fit → 4 fits → remainder 1 → 2 doesn't fit → 1 fits

Result: 10110101
